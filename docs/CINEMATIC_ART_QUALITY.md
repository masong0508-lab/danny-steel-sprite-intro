# Danny Steel – cinematic art-quality gate

This checklist governs the **real** GBA Mode-4 engine and screenshot
workflow. It is not a request to produce standalone concept art.

## Direction

- Every shot tells the story through a **specific subject**, a foreground,
  a grounded middle distance and an inhabited background. No anonymous flat
  squares standing in for a hospital, city, roof or public space.
- Establish **warm practical light / cool ambient shadows** before adding
  texture. The narrative moment determines the dominant light source:
  café windows, a sunset behind an easel, streetlamps, or the clinic's
  actual ceiling fixture. Chroma is purposeful, not random saturation.
- Prefer silhouette readability and proportions to micro-details. Heads,
  necks, shoulders, sleeves, connected arms, legs, waist and shoes must
  join. No hovering feet, circular ball torsos, egg-shaped sleeve pads,
  black horizontal arm-rib bands, detached hands or inconsistent
  character scale between cuts.
- Cut on dialogue **meaning**, not simply on page count; gestures and
  important objects remain in the same world positions. Avoid abrupt
  scale jumps. A fast reader still gets the authored camera beat; B still
  skips.
- Backgrounds should feel lived-in with grounded props and physical
  shadows. Preserve the room's identity between close-ups and wide
  framings. Keep characters clear of the bottom dialogue matte.
- In the missing-Mara chapter, the cafe retains its counter, lamps and
  empty outside chair: routine interrupted rather than an unverified
  claim of death. The rescue safehouse keeps the same curtained window,
  wall sconce and floor rug through the closed/open door and reunion
  shots. Treat these as spatial continuity anchors, not disposable art.
- The medical follow-up has an actual clinic approach: fixed glazed
  doors, foyer lighting, projecting cross, sheltered bench and reflective
  pavement; dialogue cuts from the establishment to Mara and then
  Danny without inventing a separate environment. During an unresolved
  failed rescue, an alternate grounded script and single-actor shot
  keep Mara absent; the scene never implies her unverified recovery.
- Never present a mockup as an integrated game screenshot. The gallery
  under `docs/screenshots/` is generated by the actual C renderer and
  the headless emulator.

## Technical hard limits

- GBA framebuffer: exactly **240 × 160 pixels** in the pre-existing
  64-color shared palette; palette 0–31 remain game-owned, 32–63 are the
  existing high-detail cinema tones.
- The high-detail pixel page is shared with the title/menu; no second
  bitmap, full-frame portrait atlas, heap allocations, high-frequency
  effect layers or giant literal image arrays.
- Scene effects must be bounded deterministic draw commands. Prefer
  short integer and rect/line operations, clipped through hd_canvas.
  The Old Quarter's exterior scenes use a small number of deterministic
  rain streaks and anchored puddle reflections, drawn between the
  backdrop and actors. The rain moves over time but cannot drift the
  architecture or spawn new bitmaps.
  The dusk backdrop is now rendered as six contiguous stripes rather
  than 160 per-line rectangles (154 fewer draw-call setups per shot),
  preserving its original sky/ground cutoffs and palette values.
- New cinematic composition must not change save-state logic, story
  flags, dialogue timing or branching for Mara's rescue and endings.
- The cinematic staging test checks guards, palette, portrait identities,
  connected contrasting cuffs and hands, expression variance,
  story-dependent casting, prop continuity and reachable cut points.
  Screenshot capture inspects the *actual* rendered shots.
- Keep the existing per-scene render-cost probe; host microseconds are
  not emulated GBA FPS.

## Reference-led blue-hour art pass

The art direction draws composition lessons from official reference material:

- [Eastward media gallery](https://eastwardgame.com/media/): articulated
  vertical architecture beside quiet traversable ground, recessed doors,
  selective clusters that describe a material instead of evenly spread noise.
- [The Last Night by Odd Tales](https://oddtales.net/thelastnight/): unequal
  building masses, cool ambient darkness and light attached to a visible
  practical source. Modern volumetric effects are not imported into the GBA.
- [EggNut on Backbone's art direction](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/developer-eggnut-highlights-how-they-created-backbone-s-dystopian-noir-2-5d-pixel-art-style?lang=en-US):
  establish the story, scene tone and consistent world rules before details.

These are our design interpretations, not copied art or assets. The actual
implementation uses the existing palette slots and production C renderer.

The pass reshapes all five stage actors and portraits, with sloped shoulders,
connected sleeves, trouser legs, distinct hair, tapered jaws and smaller eyes.
Slate and burgundy material ramps sit behind warm practical lights and skin.
Fourteen existing town diamonds are repainted in place: broken wet setts,
connected moss, water and slate roof courses. Their ROM footprint is unchanged.
Foreground buildings that cover Danny now become low architectural
cross-sections. Their footprint and collision remain unchanged, and Potheads
keeps its sign attached to a physical pier. The cutaway and depth insertion
follow his interpolated position during a step. Regression captures check
40 static views and 24 walking frames against his actual coat and skin pixels.

The normal and missing-person café now share one facade painter with the same
cornice, upper vents, awning, counter and cake stand. The empty chair stands
beside its table. Interiors use a recessed night window and broad floorboards;
their walls no longer compete with the characters through uniform cyan light.
The rescue's intermediate frames place Mara in front of the door after she
steps out. Hall reflections are behind the people, and her walk stops at the
contact position. The memorial retains Mara and the clerk, with subdued light
planes rather than intersecting bright lines; Danny does not appear alive.

## Studio presentation and control feedback

The six studio pages now explain the real editor state with a consistent
rule: amber marks the edited selection, and green marks playback.

| Page | Completed improvement |
|---|---|
| SONG | Tempo and feel fields have separate bounds from the turntable. The record stops when preview stops, and the footer correctly identifies field selection and editing controls. |
| NOTES | Selected columns retain every pitch-grid line. Playback markers sit beside notes; rests remain visible. The small level strips reflect the existing global feel setting, without inventing a per-note velocity editor. |
| ORDER | Each phrase previews the saved melody at its actual transposition. These are musical values, rather than decorative random bars. |
| LYRICS | Four cue choices, a cue number and a word underline follow the existing eight-step phrase. The cue remains separate from the instrumental audio. |
| CHOIR | OFF, FIFTH, OCTAVE and HIGH expose the editable harmony setting. The four voice patterns agree with the sequencer's actual note, harmony, bass and beat events. |
| LIVE | Current part and step, arrangement labels and voice activity reflect playback. A silent step displays silent channels. |

Drawing these screens changes neither the saved patch nor the sequencer's
position. The renderer regression checks 384 combinations of mood, harmony,
part and step against actual musical events, along with stopped transport,
pitch previews, selected grid lines, palette bounds and framebuffer guards.

The clearer interface also removes unnecessary painting. Three page bands
and thin panel borders replace overlapping full-page and full-panel fills.
That isolated change preserves the six images byte for byte and eliminates
46,536 commanded rectangle pixels per frame. Including the complete visual
pass, rectangle-write area falls by 42–51% across the six tabs. This is a
measure of drawing work, not an emulator FPS result.

## Actual emulator cadence

`tools/frame_cadence.c` runs the built ROM in mGBA, enters New Game, and
measures displayed Mode-4 page changes over 600 emulated video frames in
three states: the Potheads opening, overhead town and first-person town.
Companion captures verify the actual route. The workflow publishes the
source SHA and measurements in
[`screenshots/emulator_cadence.txt`](screenshots/emulator_cadence.txt).

The continuation after PR #14 produced the following **local ARM-build
measurements**. Both baseline and candidate were rebuilt with Debian ARM
GCC 14.2.1 and the official devkitPro CRT/specs, then run in mGBA 0.10.5.
The rates use elapsed emulated video time. This controlled compiler comparison
is separate from the downloaded production-ROM comparison below.

| Sampled scene | Local PR #14 source rebuild | Local candidate | Candidate presentation interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Potheads opening | 14.932 page flips/s | 19.909 page flips/s | 3 video frames |
| Overhead town | 9.955 page flips/s | 14.932 page flips/s | 4 video frames |
| First-person town | 1.394 page flips/s | 7.466 page flips/s | 8 video frames / 133.942 ms |

Both production CI workflows passed on the merged source, and the downloaded
860,972-byte release ROM was then checked in the same mGBA 0.10.5 setup as the
previous downloaded release. That published-to-published comparison is:

| Sampled scene | Published PR #14 ROM | Published PR #15 ROM |
|---|---:|---:|
| Potheads opening | 14.932 page flips/s | 19.909 page flips/s |
| Overhead town | 9.955 page flips/s | 14.932 page flips/s |
| First-person town | 1.493 page flips/s | 8.461 page flips/s |

The production first-person interval falls from 41 to 7 video frames:
686.451 ms to 117.199 ms. The earlier 1.394 figure belongs to the local
baseline rebuild, not the downloaded PR #14 binary. The
[published-ROM comparison](screenshots/published_rom_comparison.txt) records
both file hashes and raw measurements. These are sampled presentation rates,
including flips to identical content; they do not establish sustained 30 FPS
or performance on physical hardware.
See [the performance gate](GBA_PERFORMANCE_GATE.md) for timing, input,
memory and exact-image validation details.

For historical comparison, the earlier **PR #13** published ROM measured
9.955 opening and 4.977 overhead-town page flips/s in mGBA 0.10.5. Those
figures belong to the build before the art pass, not the starting baseline
of the current continuation.

The canvas drawing primitives use ARM code copied to IWRAM by the existing
devkitARM startup. Rectangle fills align their destination and write packed
four-pixel words; full-width rectangles become one contiguous span. Tiny
fills keep byte stores, and scale-one text writes clipped pixels directly.
This preserves every palette index and uses the same implementation in host
captures. The independent span test checks 48,412 rectangles, clipping and
buffer alignment, with guard bytes around the output. The ARM linker gate
counts executable IWRAM as well as static data and leaves at least 8 KiB for
the stack. The emulator report, rather than the host test's runtime, determines
whether this improves presentation cadence.

Town diamonds also copy their visible ROM spans directly from IWRAM code.
Matching source/destination alignment permits word and halfword transfers;
odd and clipped spans retain a byte-safe fallback. This removes more than
2,300 tiny library-copy calls in the sampled storefront view without changing
the 7,168 atlas bytes. Independent pre-change tile hashes and 25,088 full-frame
crop/guard comparisons protect every material, alignment and clipped edge.
The unchanged person and tree sprites clip their bounds once before their
ARM/IWRAM transparency loops, rather than checking screen bounds per pixel.

The first-person follow-up retains the shipped image while reusing rays
with identical quantized directions, avoiding floor work hidden behind
walls, and placing the measured pixel loops in ARM/IWRAM. It preserves
3,456 complete reference frames and passes 136,140 independent floor/wall
depth comparisons. The 240-column view, scene details and palette remain.

Presentation and simulation now have separate responsibilities. VBlank
records video-time input into a bounded 64-sample queue; the main thread
processes those samples in order and then paints. Whole-page DMA3 copies
finish before a completed hidden page can be published. This keeps short
presses and elapsed story time from disappearing during a slow draw, while
visible response still depends on when that draw completes. The IRQ only
extends the music clock; musical events are still sequenced on the main
thread.
